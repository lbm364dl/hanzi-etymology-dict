# Comprehensive Bibliography: Chinese Character Etymology Reference Works

This document catalogs every significant reference work for Chinese character etymology -- classical, modern, English-language, Japanese, and digital -- for the purpose of building the most comprehensive open-source Chinese character etymology database possible.

---

## I. CLASSICAL CHINESE REFERENCES (PRE-MODERN)

### 1. Shuowen Jiezi (說文解字)
- **Full title:** 說文解字 (Explaining Graphs and Analyzing Characters)
- **Author:** Xu Shen (許慎, c. 58 -- c. 148 CE)
- **Year:** Completed c. 100 CE, presented to the throne in 121 CE
- **Publisher (modern standard edition):** Zhonghua Book Company (中華書局), 1963 reprint of Xu Xuan (徐鉉) recension; numerous subsequent reprints
- **ISBN (one modern edition):** 978-7-101-00254-8 (Zhonghua shuju, based on Da Xu Ben)
- **Characters covered:** 9,353 primary characters + 1,163 graphic variants, organized under 540 radicals in small seal script (小篆). Modern editions list 9,831 characters and 1,279 variants.
- **Etymology info:** The foundational work of Chinese graphemics. Provides structural analysis of each character using the Six Principles (六書): pictographic (象形), ideographic (指事), semantic compound (會意), phono-semantic compound (形聲), derivative cognate (轉注), and phonetic loan (假借). Each entry gives the seal-script form, a definition, and an explanation of the character's construction.
- **Digital availability:**
  - Full text on [ctext.org](https://ctext.org/shuo-wen-jie-zi) with API access
  - Dedicated search at [shuowen.org](https://www.shuowen.org/)
  - GitHub: [shuowenjiezi/shuowen](https://github.com/shuowenjiezi/shuowen) (Apache 2.0)
  - [Internet Archive](https://archive.org/details/Shuowen_Jiezi)
  - Included in Academia Sinica [Xiaoxuetang database](https://xiaoxue.iis.sinica.edu.tw/)
- **Scholarly reputation:** The single most important dictionary in the history of Chinese lexicography. All subsequent character etymology work references it. It is the earliest extant systematic analysis of character structure.
- **Known issues:** Written before oracle bone and bronze inscriptions were discovered. Many etymologies are therefore incorrect by modern paleographic standards. Xu Shen only had access to small seal script; his analyses of character origins are often folk etymologies. Must be cross-referenced with modern paleographic scholarship.

### 2. Duan Yucai's Commentary on the Shuowen (段玉裁《說文解字注》)
- **Full title:** 說文解字注 (Commentary on the Shuowen Jiezi)
- **Author:** Duan Yucai (段玉裁, 1735--1815)
- **Year:** Completed 1807, published 1815 (shortly before Duan's death after 30 years of work)
- **Publisher (modern edition):** Shanghai Guji Chubanshe (上海古籍出版社); also Zhonghua Book Company
- **Characters covered:** All characters in the Shuowen (~9,353) plus additional analysis
- **Etymology info:** The most detailed traditional commentary on the Shuowen. Duan quotes extensively from Han-period literature to trace the expansion of original character meanings, corrects many of Xu Shen's statements, and provides deep phonological analysis. He demonstrated that characters sharing a phonetic component belong to the same rhyme group (a principle later central to Karlgren's work).
- **Digital availability:**
  - Included in [shuowen.org](https://www.shuowen.org/) lookup
  - [ctext.org](https://ctext.org/) (within Shuowen apparatus)
  - No standalone open structured dataset
- **Scholarly reputation:** Universally regarded as the most important and detailed traditional commentary on the Shuowen. One scholar stated "it has been 1,700 years since a work of the same quality appeared." Still the most common edition used by students of the Shuowen.
- **Known issues:** Still limited by pre-paleographic evidence. Some of Duan's own corrections have themselves been corrected by later scholarship.

### 3. Other Major Shuowen Commentaries (Qing Dynasty)

#### 3a. Gui Fu (桂馥)《說文解字義證》
- **Full title:** 說文解字義證 (Verification of Meanings in the Shuowen Jiezi)
- **Author:** Gui Fu (桂馥, 1736--1805)
- **Year:** Qing dynasty; modern reprint by Qilu Publishing House (齊魯書社), Jinan, 1987
- **Etymology info:** Focuses on clarifying the meanings of characters through systematic cross-references to classical allusions and archaeological finds. Follows the analytical exegesis model of Xu Kai.

#### 3b. Wang Yun (王筠)《說文句讀》
- **Full title:** 說文句讀 (Shuowen Sentence Readings)
- **Author:** Wang Yun (王筠, 1784--1854)
- **Etymology info:** Analysis of textual variants and syntactic reading of the Shuowen text.

#### 3c. Zhu Junsheng (朱駿聲)《說文通訓定聲》
- **Full title:** 說文通訓定聲 (Comprehensive Glosses and Fixed Sounds of the Shuowen)
- **Author:** Zhu Junsheng (朱駿聲, 1788--1858)
- **Year:** Completed 1833, published 1870; 18 juan
- **Characters covered:** All ~9,000 Shuowen characters + 7,000 additional characters, organized into 1,137 phonetic series grouped under 18 Shijing rhyme groups
- **Etymology info:** A phonetic reorganization of the Shuowen that groups characters by shared phonetic elements. Provides very detailed notes on rhyming, semantics, and interchangeable characters. Anticipated the structure of Karlgren's Grammata Serica Recensa by a century.
- **Digital availability:** Available on [ctext.org](https://ctext.org/wiki.pl?if=en&res=247132) and [Internet Archive](https://archive.org/details/02076590.cn)
- **Scholarly reputation:** One of the "four great Shuowen commentaries" of the Qing dynasty. Particularly valued for its phonological organization and its insights into semantic extension through sound.

### 4. Erya (爾雅) and Commentaries
- **Full title:** 爾雅 (Approaching the Correct/Refined)
- **Author:** Unknown; compilation spanning late 4th to early 2nd century BCE, with possible core material from the 6th century BCE
- **Year:** Pre-Qin to early Han dynasty
- **Characters covered:** Not a character dictionary per se, but a thesaurus/glossary organizing words by semantic category (19 sections)
- **Etymology info:** China's earliest surviving dictionary. Organizes words thematically (heaven, earth, plants, animals, implements, etc.) and provides glosses. Important for understanding classical word meanings and semantic fields.
- **Major commentaries:**
  - Guo Pu (郭璞, 276--324):《爾雅注》-- earliest surviving commentary (c. 310 CE)
  - Xing Bing (邢昺):《爾雅疏》(c. 1000 CE) -- standard subcommentary
  - Hao Yixing (郝懿行):《爾雅義疏》(1808--1822) -- Qing-era comprehensive study
  - Shao Jinhan (邵晉涵):《爾雅正義》-- Qing-era critical edition
- **Digital availability:** Full text on [ctext.org](https://ctext.org/er-ya)
- **Scholarly reputation:** One of the Thirteen Classics (十三經). Foundational for Chinese lexicography and the 訓詁 (exegetical) tradition.
- **Known issues:** Not a character-form dictionary; primarily about word meanings and glosses. Authorship and dating are uncertain.

### 5. Yupian (玉篇)
- **Full title:** 玉篇 (Jade Chapters)
- **Author:** Gu Yewang (顧野王, 519--581)
- **Year:** 543 CE (Liang dynasty)
- **Characters covered:** 12,158 character entries organized under 542 radicals
- **Etymology info:** The first kaishu (regular script) dictionary organized by radicals. Each entry focuses on pronunciation (using fanqie) and meaning. Important for understanding character readings and definitions in the medieval period.
- **Digital availability:** Available on [ctext.org](https://ctext.org/); fragments and later recensions are preserved
- **Scholarly reputation:** The second-oldest Chinese dictionary after the Shuowen jiezi. Important transitional work between the seal-script-based Shuowen and later radical dictionaries.
- **Known issues:** The original work is lost; only Song-dynasty revisions survive (the "Revised Yupian" 大廣益會玉篇 of 1013). The surviving version was significantly expanded and altered.

### 6. Zihui (字彙) and Zhengzitong (正字通)

#### 6a. Zihui (字彙)
- **Full title:** 字彙 (Collection of Characters)
- **Author:** Mei Yingzuo (梅膺祚)
- **Year:** 1615 (late Ming dynasty)
- **Characters covered:** ~33,000 characters organized under 214 radicals
- **Etymology info:** Mei Yingzuo's great innovation was reducing the 540 Shuowen radicals to 214 and introducing indexing by residual stroke count. Characters are written in contemporary regular script rather than seal script. This is a practical dictionary rather than an etymological one, but its 214-radical system became the standard.
- **Digital availability:** [ctext.org](https://ctext.org/wiki.pl?if=en&remap=gb&res=631239)
- **Scholarly reputation:** "The first important lexicographic advance" in centuries. Established the 214-radical system later adopted by the Kangxi Dictionary.

#### 6b. Zhengzitong (正字通)
- **Full title:** 正字通 (Comprehensive [Dictionary] of Orthodox Characters)
- **Author:** Zhang Zilie (張自烈, 1597--1673)
- **Year:** Late Ming dynasty
- **Etymology info:** An abbreviated and corrected version of the Zihui. Strengthened the use of the 214-radical system. Served as an important stepping stone to the Kangxi Dictionary.
- **Known issues:** Contains some errors inherited from and added to the Zihui.

### 7. Kangxi Dictionary (康熙字典)
- **Full title:** 御製康熙字典 (Imperially Commissioned Kangxi Dictionary)
- **Authors:** Zhang Yushu (張玉書) and Chen Tingjing (陳廷敬) et al., commissioned by Emperor Kangxi
- **Year:** 1716
- **Characters covered:** 47,043 character entries (supplemented edition: 49,030 including graphic variants)
- **Etymology info:** Provides definitions, pronunciation via fanqie, and citations from classical literature for each character. Not an etymological dictionary per se, but the standard reference for character meanings and readings for 300 years. Uses the 214-radical system.
- **Digital availability:**
  - [ctext.org](https://ctext.org/kangxi-zidian) (7th Zhonghua edition, fully searchable)
  - GitHub: [samsonhoi/kangxi-dictionary](https://github.com/samsonhoi/kangxi-dictionary) (MIT, 48,710 records in XLSX)
  - Various online lookup sites
- **Scholarly reputation:** The preeminent standard Chinese character dictionary of the imperial era. Still widely referenced.
- **Known issues:** Contains known errors that were partially addressed in a 1831 supplement. Many of the ~47,000 characters are obscure variants or hapax legomena.

### 8. Liushu Tong (六書通)
- **Full title:** 六書通 (Compendium of the Six Principles of Writing)
- **Author:** Bi Yuan (畢沅, 1730--1797), compiler; builds on earlier Yuan-dynasty works by Yang Huan (楊桓)
- **Year:** Qing dynasty (Bi Yuan edition); Yang Huan's precursor 六書統 presented to throne in 1308
- **Etymology info:** Classifies characters using the traditional Six Principles system (六書). Provides multiple historical script forms for each character: ancient script (古文), large seal script (大篆), bell-and-tripod script (鐘鼎文), and small seal script (小篆).
- **Digital availability:** Character forms from the Liushutong are available on [hanziyuan.net](https://hanziyuan.net/)
- **Scholarly reputation:** Important reference for historical script forms and character classification. The Bi Yuan edition is the standard.

### 9. Shen Jianshi (沈兼士)《廣韻聲系》
- **Full title:** 廣韻聲系 (Sound System of the Guangyun)
- **Author:** Shen Jianshi (沈兼士, 1887--1947)
- **Year:** 1945 (completed); published 1960 by 文字改革出版社 (Written Language Reform Press), Beijing
- **Etymology info:** Arranges the characters of the Guangyun rhyme dictionary by their phonetic components, creating phonetic series that reveal sound-meaning relationships. Important bridge between traditional Chinese philology and modern historical phonology. Shen also wrote the influential 1935 article on the "Right-side Component Theory" (右文說), exploring how shared phonetic components carry semantic information.
- **Scholarly reputation:** A landmark work that systematized phonetic series analysis. Highly influential for subsequent work on word families and phonetic-semantic relationships.

## II. MODERN CHINESE-LANGUAGE ETYMOLOGY WORKS (20th--21st CENTURY)

### 10. Xu Zhongshu (徐中舒) ed.《甲骨文字典》(Oracle Bone Script Dictionary)
- **Full title:** 甲骨文字典 (Oracle Bone Script Dictionary)
- **Editor-in-chief:** Xu Zhongshu (徐中舒, 1898--1991)
- **Year:** 1st edition 1988; 2nd edition 1998; 3rd edition c. 2014
- **Publisher:** Sichuan Cishu Chubanshe (四川辭書出版社), Chengdu
- **ISBN:** 978-7-806-82292-2 (one edition); 978-7-806-82232-6 (2nd edition)
- **Characters covered:** ~1,500+ oracle bone characters with detailed analysis
- **Etymology info:** Provides the oracle bone glyph form, glyph analysis, textual citations from oracle bone inscriptions, and interpretive notes for each character. Organized following the Shuowen radical order.
- **Digital availability:** Available on Internet Archive; various PDF copies circulate
- **Scholarly reputation:** "Even though it has been published for more than thirty years, Xu's 1988 dictionary is still the most widely used and authoritative oracle bone dictionary in the field." The standard reference.
- **Known issues:** Does not include oracle bone discoveries made after publication. Newer finds require supplementation.

### 11. Li Xiaoding (李孝定)《甲骨文字集釋》(Oracle Bone Characters: Collected Interpretations)
- **Full title:** 甲骨文字集釋 (Collected Interpretations of Oracle Bone Characters)
- **Author:** Li Xiaoding (李孝定)
- **Year:** 1965 (completed); published by Institute of History and Philology, Academia Sinica
- **Characters covered:** 14 volumes, 8,000+ pages; covers 1,703 correctly interpreted characters, 797 with variant interpretations, and 184 awaiting further examination
- **Etymology info:** A "compilation-type" dictionary that collects and synthesizes interpretations from various scholars for each oracle bone character. Provides extensive scholarly apparatus for each entry.
- **Scholarly reputation:** Was the most comprehensive compilation of oracle bone character scholarship prior to Yu Xingwu's Jiagu Wenzi Gulin. Remains an important reference.

### 12. Yu Xingwu (于省吾) ed.《甲骨文字詁林》(Exegetical Forest of Oracle Bone Characters)
- **Full title:** 甲骨文字詁林 (Exegetical Forest of Oracle Bone Characters)
- **Editor:** Yu Xingwu (于省吾, 1896--1984) and Yao Xiaosui (姚孝遂)
- **Year:** 1996
- **Publisher:** Zhonghua Book Company (中華書局), Beijing
- **Characters covered:** Comprehensive compilation of all scholarly interpretations of oracle bone characters
- **Etymology info:** Summarizes the divergent interpretations of various oracle bone scholars, providing a comprehensive view of how each character has been analyzed by different researchers over the decades.
- **Scholarly reputation:** The most comprehensive compilation of oracle bone scholarship. Essential reference for any serious paleographic research.

### 13. Li Zongkun (李宗焜)《新甲骨文編》(New Oracle Bone Script Compilation)
- **Full title:** 新甲骨文編 (New Oracle Bone Script Compilation)
- **Author:** Li Zongkun (李宗焜)
- **Year:** 2012 (approximate)
- **Publisher:** Zhonghua Book Company (中華書局)
- **Etymology info:** Organized according to the Shuowen radical sequence. Collects Shang and Western Zhou oracle bone script forms. Divided into main compilation, combined characters, and appendices. Updated with newer archaeological discoveries.
- **Scholarly reputation:** An important update to earlier oracle bone compilations, incorporating more recently discovered material.

### 14. Rong Geng (容庚)《金文編》(Bronze Inscription Compilation)
- **Full title:** 金文編 (Compilation of Bronze Inscriptions)
- **Author:** Rong Geng (容庚, 1894--1983)
- **Year:** 1st edition 1925; revised 1939; 4th edition 1985
- **Publisher:** Zhonghua Book Company (中華書局)
- **ISBN:** 978-7-101-00533-4
- **Characters covered:** Comprehensive documentation of bronze script characters from Shang and Zhou dynasty vessels
- **Etymology info:** Systematic collection and organization of bronze inscription character forms. The term "bronze script" (金文) itself was established by Rong Geng in this work. Essential for understanding character forms in the Bronze Age period between oracle bone and seal script.
- **Digital availability:** [Internet Archive](https://archive.org/details/chinese-paleography-collection) (within paleography collection)
- **Scholarly reputation:** "One of the most basic reference works in the library of any scholar of bronze inscriptions." Foundational for the field.

### 15. Gu Wenzi Gulin (古文字詁林) (Exegetical Forest of Ancient Characters)
- **Full title:** 古文字詁林 (Exegetical Forest of Ancient Characters)
- **Editors:** Li Pu (李圃) et al.
- **Year:** 1999--2004 (12 volumes)
- **Publisher:** Shanghai Jiaoyu Chubanshe (上海教育出版社)
- **Characters covered:** Comprehensive -- collects over 10,000 historical documents about ancient Chinese characters
- **Etymology info:** A massive compilation that brings together annotations and interpretations of ancient Chinese characters from Chinese historical texts, arranged for reader convenience with multiple typeface reproductions. Covers oracle bone, bronze, seal, and other ancient script forms.
- **Digital availability:** [Internet Archive](https://archive.org/details/guwenzi-gulin) (scanned)
- **Scholarly reputation:** A national key ancient books compilation project, undertaken by East China Normal University over 14 years. The most comprehensive single reference for ancient character scholarship.

### 16. Hanyu Da Zidian (漢語大字典) (Great Chinese Character Dictionary)
- **Full title:** 漢語大字典 (Great Chinese Character Dictionary)
- **Editor-in-chief:** Xu Zhongshu (徐中舒) et al. (400+ editors)
- **Year:** 1st edition 1986--1989 (8 volumes); 2nd edition 2010 (9 volumes)
- **Publisher:** Sichuan Cishu Chubanshe (四川辭書出版社) and Chongwen Shuju (崇文書局)
- **ISBN (2nd ed.):** 978-7-540-31532-5
- **Characters covered:** 54,678 entries (1st ed.); 60,370 entries (2nd ed.)
- **Etymology info:** For each character: definitions, pronunciations, citations from classical literature, historical glyph forms (oracle bone, bronze, seal script where available). The most comprehensive single-volume Chinese character dictionary.
- **Digital availability:** Available on [Internet Archive](https://archive.org/details/xu-zhong-shu-hanyu-da-zidian-1986-1989); also available in some digital dictionary apps
- **Scholarly reputation:** The standard large-scale Chinese character dictionary. The go-to reference for character meanings, readings, and citations. Superseded only by specialized dictionaries for specific script types.
- **Known issues:** The first edition contains some errors corrected in the second edition. Coverage of ancient character forms is not as deep as specialized paleographic dictionaries.

### 17. Zhonghua Zihai (中華字海)
- **Full title:** 中華字海 (Sea of Chinese Characters)
- **Editors:** Leng Yulong (冷玉龍) and Wei Yixin (韋一心)
- **Year:** 1994
- **Publisher:** Zhonghua Book Company (中華書局) and Zhongguo Youyi Chuban Gongsi
- **Characters covered:** 85,568 characters -- the largest Chinese character dictionary in print
- **Etymology info:** Primarily a character index rather than an etymological dictionary. First section covers ~50,000 characters from earlier dictionaries (Shuowen, Yupian, Guangyun, Jiyun, Kangxi, etc.). Second section adds characters from Buddhist texts, Dunhuang manuscripts, Song/Yuan/Ming/Qing rare characters, dialectal characters, scientific neologisms, and characters from Japanese kanji and Korean hanja.
- **Scholarly reputation:** The largest character collection ever published in print. Important for identifying obscure and variant characters.
- **Known issues:** Many entries have minimal etymological information. The dictionary prioritizes breadth of coverage over depth of analysis.

### 18. Wang Li (王力)《同源字典》(Dictionary of Cognate Words)
- **Full title:** 同源字典 (Dictionary of Cognate Words)
- **Author:** Wang Li (王力, 1900--1986)
- **Year:** 1982
- **Publisher:** Shangwu Yinshuguan (商務印書館), Beijing
- **Etymology info:** Collects cognate word pairs -- words with related pronunciation and meaning -- and analyzes their phonological connections. Demonstrates that slight pronunciation differences between cognate words follow systematic rules related to Old Chinese syllable structure. Essential for understanding word families and phonetic-semantic relationships.
- **Digital availability:** A database version has been created for computational research (see: Springer, 2022)
- **Scholarly reputation:** A landmark work by one of the pioneers of modern Chinese linguistics. Foundational for understanding Chinese word families and morphological processes.

### 19. Wang Li (王力)《王力古漢語字典》(Wang Li's Dictionary of Old Chinese)
- **Full title:** 王力古漢語字典 (Wang Li's Dictionary of Old Chinese)
- **Author:** Wang Li (王力) et al.
- **Year:** 2000
- **Publisher:** Zhonghua Book Company (中華書局)
- **ISBN:** 978-7-101-01219-6
- **Etymology info:** Features synonym discrimination, cognate word analysis, and other academic modules for classical Chinese. Integrates Wang Li's lifetime of research into historical Chinese linguistics.

### 20. Qiu Xigui (裘錫圭)《文字學概要》(Outline of Chinese Writing)
- **Full title:** 文字學概要 (Outline of Chinese Writing / Chinese Writing)
- **Author:** Qiu Xigui (裘錫圭, 1935--2025)
- **Year:** 1988 (Beijing edition); 1994 (Taipei revised edition)
- **Publisher:** Shangwu Yinshuguan (商務印書館)
- **English translation:** "Chinese Writing" (2000), translated by Gilbert L. Mattos and Jerry Norman, published by the Society for the Study of Early China / Institute of East Asian Studies, UC Berkeley. ISBN: 978-1-557-29071-7
- **Etymology info:** Comprehensive theoretical framework for understanding the Chinese writing system. Covers the principles of character formation, the history of Chinese script from oracle bones to modern forms, and the relationship between writing and language. The English translation combines the 1988 Beijing and 1994 Taipei editions.
- **Digital availability:** English translation PDF available at [starlingdb.org](https://starlingdb.org/Texts/Students/Qiu%20Xigui/Chinese%20Writing%20(2000).pdf)
- **Scholarly reputation:** "The single most influential study of Chinese palaeography" and "universally acclaimed to be the definitive overview" of the field (Edward Shaughnessy). Essential reading for anyone working on Chinese character etymology.
- **Known issues:** The English translation was completed before some of the most significant bamboo manuscript discoveries of the 2000s.

### 21. Ji Xusheng (季旭昇)《說文新證》(New Evidence for the Shuowen)
- **Full title:** 說文新證 (New Evidence for the Shuowen Jiezi)
- **Author:** Ji Xusheng (季旭昇)
- **Year:** 1st edition 2002; 2nd edition 2014
- **Publisher:** Yiwen Yinshuguan (藝文印書館), Taipei
- **Characters covered:** Covers Shuowen characters with updated analysis based on modern archaeological evidence
- **Etymology info:** Takes each Shuowen entry and provides "new evidence" from oracle bone, bronze, Warring States bamboo/silk, and other paleographic sources. Effectively a modern correction and expansion of the Shuowen using 20th/21st-century archaeological discoveries.
- **Digital availability:** PDF available on [Scribd](https://www.scribd.com/document/376369077/)
- **Scholarly reputation:** Highly regarded in the field of Chinese paleography. Bridges classical Shuowen scholarship with modern archaeological evidence. Essential for any project that uses Shuowen data.

### 22. He Linyi (何琳儀)《戰國古文字典》(Dictionary of Warring States Ancient Characters)
- **Full title:** 戰國古文字典 -- 戰國文字聲系 (Dictionary of Warring States Ancient Characters -- Phonetic Series of Warring States Characters)
- **Author:** He Linyi (何琳儀)
- **Year:** 1998
- **Publisher:** Zhonghua Book Company (中華書局), Beijing
- **Etymology info:** Organizes Warring States period characters by phonetic series. Covers glyph studies, subdivision of regional text systems, and glyph evolution during the Warring States period (475--221 BCE). Important for understanding the great diversity of character forms before Qin standardization.
- **Scholarly reputation:** A key reference for Warring States paleography, filling the chronological gap between bronze inscriptions and Qin seal script.

### 23. Gao Ming (高明)《古文字類編》(Ancient Character Compilation by Category)
- **Full title:** 古文字類編 (Categorized Compilation of Ancient Characters)
- **Author:** Gao Ming (高明)
- **Year:** 1980
- **Publisher:** Zhonghua Book Company (中華書局), Beijing
- **Etymology info:** Reference tool organizing ancient character forms by category. Serves as a lookup reference for scholars studying character variants across different periods and writing systems (oracle bone, bronze, seal).
- **Scholarly reputation:** An important standard reference in Chinese paleography, widely cited alongside other major compilations.

### 24. Tang Yuhui (湯餘惠) et al.《戰國文字編》(Warring States Script Compilation)
- **Full title:** 戰國文字編 (Warring States Script Compilation)
- **Authors:** Tang Yuhui (湯餘惠), Lai Bingwei, Xu Zaiguo, Wu Liangbao
- **Year:** 2001
- **Publisher:** Fujian Renmin Chubanshe (福建人民出版社)
- **Etymology info:** Compilation of character forms from the Warring States period, organized systematically. Complements He Linyi's work with a different organizational approach.

### 25. Gu Yankui (谷衍奎)《漢字源流字典》(Dictionary of Chinese Character Etymology and Evolution)
- **Full title:** 漢字源流字典 (Dictionary of Chinese Character Origins and Development)
- **Author:** Gu Yankui (谷衍奎, b. 1937)
- **Year:** 2008 (standard edition); 漢字源流大字典 expanded edition 2023
- **Publisher:** Yuwen Chubanshe (語文出版社); expanded edition by Shangwu Yinshuguan (商務印書館) ISBN: 978-7-100-21633-3
- **Characters covered:** 11,000+ characters
- **Etymology info:** Provides multidimensional analysis for each character: pronunciation, form, construction method (using Six Principles theory), illustrations of historical forms, original meaning, semantic evolution, and character composition relationships. Traces the development from source to current form.
- **Scholarly reputation:** Well-regarded as a practical etymology reference that bridges classical and modern analysis. Written by a senior Chinese language teacher and Peking University graduate.

### 26. Jiaguwen Heji (甲骨文合集) (Collected Oracle Bone Inscriptions)
- **Full title:** 甲骨文合集 (Collected Oracle Bone Inscriptions)
- **Editors:** Guo Moruo (郭沫若, chief editor) and Hu Houxuan (胡厚宣, general editor)
- **Year:** 1978--1982 (13 volumes)
- **Publisher:** Zhonghua Book Company (中華書局)
- **Content:** 41,956 selected inscriptions from over 150,000 oracle bone fragments; approximately 4,600 unique characters identified
- **Etymology info:** Not a dictionary but the standard corpus of oracle bone rubbings. Essential primary source material for all oracle bone character research.
- **Digital availability:** [Internet Archive](https://archive.org/details/06-14822-19753)
- **Scholarly reputation:** The standard comprehensive collection. All oracle bone scholarship references it by "Heji" number.

### 27. Chen Chusheng (陳初生)《金文常用字典》(Dictionary of Commonly Used Bronze Inscription Characters)
- **Full title:** 金文常用字典 (Dictionary of Commonly Used Bronze Inscription Characters)
- **Author:** Chen Chusheng (陳初生)
- **Year:** 1987; revised edition 2004
- **Publisher:** Shaanxi Renmin Chubanshe (陝西人民出版社)
- **Etymology info:** A more accessible companion to Rong Geng's comprehensive Jinwen Bian, focusing on commonly encountered bronze inscription characters with explanations of their forms and meanings.

### 28. Lin Yiguang (林義光)《文源》(Sources of Writing)

- **Author:** Lin Yiguang (林義光)
- **Year:** 1920
- **Etymology info:** Early modern study of character origins, analyzing the pictorial and conceptual bases of Chinese characters. Important precursor to modern paleographic analysis.

## III. ENGLISH-LANGUAGE SCHOLARSHIP

### 29. Wieger, Leon -- "Chinese Characters: Their Origin, Etymology, History, Classification and Signification"
- **Full title:** Chinese Characters: Their Origin, Etymology, History, Classification and Signification: A Thorough Study from Chinese Documents
- **Author:** Leon Wieger, S.J. (1856--1933); translated by L. Davrout, S.J.
- **Year:** 1st edition 1915; 2nd enlarged/revised edition 1927
- **Publisher:** Catholic Mission Press, Hsien-hsien (Ho-kien-fu); Dover reprint 1965
- **ISBN (Dover):** 978-0-486-21321-7
- **Characters covered:** ~2,300 characters classified according to 224 "primitives"; includes a lexicon of ~10,000 characters by the 214 radical system

### 30. Karlgren, Bernhard -- "Analytic Dictionary of Chinese and Sino-Japanese"
- **Full title:** Analytic Dictionary of Chinese and Sino-Japanese
- **Author:** Bernhard Karlgren (1889--1978)
- **Year:** 1923 (Paris, Paul Geuthner); Dover reprint 1980
- **ISBN (Dover):** 978-0-486-21887-8
- **Pages:** 436
- **Characters covered:** Groups characters by shared phonetic elements (xiesheng series)
- **Etymology info:** Provides IPA transcriptions for Mandarin, Cantonese, and Japanese readings. Organizes characters by phonetic component, establishing the framework for phonetic series analysis. The pioneering work of modern Chinese historical phonology applied to character analysis.
- **Digital availability:**
  - [Internet Archive](https://archive.org/details/analyticdictiona00karl) (free download)
  - Dover reprint available in print
- **Scholarly reputation:** Foundational work. Karlgren was the first to apply modern comparative method to Chinese historical phonology. This dictionary established the approach later perfected in Grammata Serica Recensa.

### 31. Karlgren, Bernhard -- "Grammata Serica Recensa"
- **Full title:** Grammata Serica Recensa
- **Author:** Bernhard Karlgren
- **Year:** 1957 (published in Bulletin of the Museum of Far Eastern Antiquities 29; reprinted 1964, 1972)
- **Publisher:** Museum of Far Eastern Antiquities, Stockholm
- **Characters covered:** Approximately 7,700 characters organized into ~1,260 phonetic series
- **Etymology info:** For each character: Middle Chinese reconstruction (c. 600 CE), Old Chinese reconstruction (pre-200 BCE), definitions, and placement within phonetic series. The phonetic series are grouped by Shijing rhyme groups. The "homorganic principle" -- that characters sharing a phonetic component had a common point of articulation -- has been central to all subsequent Old Chinese studies.
- **Digital availability:**
  - PDF at [starlingdb.org](https://starlingdb.org/Texts/Students/Karlgren,%20Bernhard/Grammata%20Serica%20Recensa%20(1964).pdf)
  - [Scribd](https://www.scribd.com/document/299367216/Grammata-Serica-Recensa-by-Bernhard-Karlgren)
  - Alphabetical rearrangement by Tor Ulving: "Dictionary of Old and Middle Chinese" (1997, ISBN 91-7346-294-2)
- **Scholarly reputation:** "The most convenient work for looking up Middle Chinese and Old Chinese reconstructions of all graphs that occur in literature from the beginning of writing down to the third century B.C." The standard reference for mid-20th-century Chinese historical phonology, now partially superseded by Baxter-Sagart but still essential.
- **Known issues:** Karlgren's reconstructions have been significantly revised by subsequent scholarship (Baxter, Sagart, Zhengzhang, Starostin). His Old Chinese vowel system and initial consonant clusters are now considered outdated.

### 32. Karlgren, Bernhard -- "Word Families in Chinese"
- **Full title:** Word Families in Chinese
- **Author:** Bernhard Karlgren
- **Year:** 1933 (Bulletin of the Museum of Far Eastern Antiquities 5, pp. 9--120)
- **Etymology info:** Demonstrated that the Chinese language at an early stage had productive morphological processes. Reconstructed etymological connections between words, including final voiced stops (*-b, *-d, *-g) in words showing connections with words having other finals. A foundational work for Chinese word-family studies.

### 33. Schuessler, Axel -- "ABC Etymological Dictionary of Old Chinese"
- **Full title:** ABC Etymological Dictionary of Old Chinese
- **Author:** Axel Schuessler
- **Year:** 2007
- **Publisher:** University of Hawaii Press
- **ISBN:** 978-0-824-82975-9
- **Characters covered:** Thousands of Old Chinese lexical items
- **Etymology info:** The first genuine etymological dictionary of Old Chinese written in any language. Places Old Chinese within the Sino-Tibetan language family with close consideration of Tibeto-Burman languages, while also examining contact with Austroasiatic, Hmong-Mien, and Kam-Tai languages. Provides cognates across multiple language families.
- **Digital availability:**
  - [JSTOR](https://www.jstor.org/stable/j.ctt6wr0n4)
  - [De Gruyter/Brill](https://www.degruyterbrill.com/document/doi/10.1515/9780824861339/html)
  - Digital EDOC version at [University of Chicago](https://edoc.uchicago.edu/edoc2013/digitaledoc_index.php) (free web access)
- **Scholarly reputation:** Historically significant as the first etymological dictionary of Old Chinese. However, as the first attempt, it has known limitations. Reviews note that "like all first etymological dictionaries of any language or language family, it is only the first step." Some etymological connections are questioned.
- **Known issues:** Some connections are subjective; treatment of language contact is incomplete; users uninformed in historical Chinese studies should be cautious with the data.

### 34. Schuessler, Axel -- "Minimal Old Chinese and Later Han Chinese"
- **Full title:** Minimal Old Chinese and Later Han Chinese: A Companion to Grammata Serica Recensa
- **Author:** Axel Schuessler
- **Year:** 2009
- **Publisher:** University of Hawaii Press
- **ISBN:** 978-0-824-83264-3
- **Etymology info:** Updates Karlgren's GSR with more current Old Chinese reconstructions, limited to features enjoying scholarly consensus ("Minimal Old Chinese" / OCM). ### 35. Baxter, William H. -- "A Handbook of Old Chinese Phonology"
- **Full title:** A Handbook of Old Chinese Phonology
- **Author:** William H. Baxter
- **Year:** 1992
- **Publisher:** Mouton de Gruyter (Trends in Linguistics, Studies and Monographs 64)
- **ISBN:** 978-3-110-12324-1
- **Pages:** 922
- **Etymology info:** Proposes a comprehensive reconstruction of Old Chinese phonology (11th--7th century BCE). Provides reconstructions for thousands of characters.

### 36. Baxter, William H. & Sagart, Laurent -- "Old Chinese: A New Reconstruction"
- **Full title:** Old Chinese: A New Reconstruction
- **Authors:** William H. Baxter and Laurent Sagart
- **Year:** 2014
- **Publisher:** Oxford University Press
- **ISBN:** 978-0-199-94537-5
- **Characters covered:** ~5,000 items with Old Chinese reconstructions
- **Etymology info:** The current state-of-the-art Old Chinese phonological reconstruction. Introduces uvular stops (following Pan Wuyun), pharyngealized initials for type-A syllables, final *-r, and complex onset clusters based on Proto-Min and early loanword evidence. Provides comprehensive reconstruction tables.
- **Digital availability:**
  - Reconstruction data freely downloadable as XLSX from [University of Michigan](https://sites.lsa.umich.edu/ocbaxtersagart/)
  - The data is widely used in [Wiktionary](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/Appendix:Baxter-Sagart_Old_Chinese_reconstruction)
- **Scholarly reputation:** The most influential current Old Chinese reconstruction system. Widely adopted in computational and lexicographic work. However, reviews identify "methodological flaws, particularly the authors' quest for total explainability, leading to a fragile system of phonetic correspondences." Criticized for Proto-Min data problems.
- **Known issues:** The system is complex and sometimes controversial; specific critiques exist regarding Proto-Min reconstruction methodology. Active debate continues in the field.

### 37. Sagart, Laurent -- "The Roots of Old Chinese"
- **Full title:** The Roots of Old Chinese
- **Author:** Laurent Sagart
- **Year:** 1999
- **Publisher:** John Benjamins (Current Issues in Linguistic Theory 184)
- **ISBN:** 978-1-556-19961-5

## IV. JAPANESE SCHOLARSHIP

### 48. Shirakawa Shizuka (白川静) -- Major Dictionaries
- **Full name:** Shirakawa Shizuka (白川静, 1910--2006)
- **Major works:**
  - **字統** (Jitou / Character Origins): A masterwork on kanji etymology, analyzing character origins through oracle bone and bronze inscription forms. Published 1984, revised 2004 by Heibonsha (平凡社).
  - **字訓** (Jikun / Character Readings): Investigates the native Japanese readings of kanji characters. Published 1987, revised 2005 by Heibonsha.
  - **字通** (Jitsuu / Character Compendium): A comprehensive kanji dictionary exploring character vocabulary and connections. Published 1996 by Heibonsha.
  - **常用字解** (Jouyou Jikai / Explanation of Commonly Used Characters): Explains the origins of approximately 2,000 daily-use kanji. Published 2003 by Heibonsha.
- **English translation:** "The Keys to the Chinese Characters" (2014), translated by Christoph Schmitz. ISBN: 978-1-503-03630-7
- **Etymology info:** Shirakawa systematized the original meaning of kanji through studying their oracle bone and bronze inscription forms. His approach, called "Shirakawa Grammatology," reveals how characters were actually constructed as descriptions of daily life in pre-Confucian, animist China. He emphasized ritual and religious contexts that other scholars had overlooked.
- **Digital availability:** The Jitou and Jitsuu are available on [JapanKnowledge](https://japanknowledge.com/en/contents/jitsu/) (subscription required). Print editions widely available.
- **Scholarly reputation:** "Received high praise from both domestic and international academia." Became a Person of Cultural Merit (1998) and received the Order of Culture (2004). His method is now "the standard method adopted at a number of Japanese schools." The [Shirakawa Shizuka Institute](https://www.ritsumei.ac.jp/research/shirakawa/eng/) at Ritsumeikan University continues his work.
- **Known issues:** Some of Shirakawa's interpretations (especially regarding ritual contexts) are controversial in Chinese academic circles. His emphasis on shamanic and ritual explanations for character origins is not universally accepted.

### 49. Todo Akiyasu (藤堂明保) -- Kanwa Dictionaries
- **Full name:** Todo Akiyasu (藤堂明保, 1915--1985)
- **Major works:**
  - **漢和大字典** (Kanwa Daijiten / Great Kanji-Japanese Dictionary): Published by Gakken (学研), 1978. Contains 20,000 headwords and 120,000 compounds.
  - **漢字源** (Kanjigen / Character Origins): Originally 1973, 5th revised edition 2011, published by Gakken Kyoiku Shuppan. A widely-used kanji etymology dictionary.
  - **漢字語源辞典** (Kanji Gogen Jiten / Kanji Etymological Dictionary)
- **Etymology info:** Todo's approach emphasizes phonetic-semantic connections and word families in Chinese. His dictionaries provide etymological explanations for each kanji based on phonological analysis and historical forms. His work was influenced by Karlgren's phonetic series approach.
- **Scholarly reputation:** A major figure in Japanese sinology and lexicography. His dictionaries remain standard references for kanji etymology in Japan.

### 50. Kato Joken (加藤常賢) -- Character Origin Studies
- **Full name:** Kato Joken (加藤常賢)
- **Major works:**
  - **漢字の起源** (Kanji no Kigen / The Origin of Chinese Characters): Published 1970 by Kadokawa Shoten, Tokyo.
  - **角川字源辞典** (Kadokawa Jigen Jiten / Kadokawa Character Origin Dictionary): Co-authored with Yamada Katsumi and Shinto Hideyuki. 2nd edition 1983, Kadokawa Shoten.
- **Etymology info:** Kato claimed to have identified very old character forms and maintained that phonetic components often point back to the original word for which a graph was devised. His methodology was utilized heavily in Kenneth Henshall's "A Guide to Remembering Japanese Characters."
- **Scholarly reputation:** An influential figure in Japanese character etymology studies, though some of his specific identifications of ancient forms are debated.

### 51. Morohashi Tetsuji (諸橋轍次) -- Dai Kan-Wa Jiten (大漢和辞典)
- **Full title:** 大漢和辞典 (Great Chinese-Japanese Dictionary)
- **Author:** Morohashi Tetsuji (諸橋轍次, 1883--1982)
- **Year:** 1955--1960 (original 13 volumes); supplements 1986 (Vol. 14) and 2000 (Vol. 15)
- **Publisher:** Taishukan (大修館書店), Tokyo
- **ISBN:** 978-4-469-03090-7 (set)
- **Characters covered:** 49,964 character entries + 530,000+ compound words (original); ~50,764 with supplements
- **Pages:** 13,757 (original 13 volumes)
- **Etymology info:** Comprehensive dictionary covering Classical Chinese and Literary Chinese vocabulary across all historical periods. For each character: definitions, readings (Chinese and Japanese), example citations from pre-Qin classics through Ming-Qing novels, Buddhist texts, medicine, law, and more. Not specifically etymological, but provides the deepest coverage of character usage across Chinese literature.
- **Digital availability:** Available on [JapanKnowledge](https://japanknowledge.com/en/contents/daikanwa/) (subscription). Print set available but expensive.
- **Scholarly reputation:** "The definitive dictionary of the Chinese characters and one of the great dictionaries of the world." Unmatched in scope and depth for Classical Chinese vocabulary.
- **Known issues:** Japanese-language only (definitions in Japanese). Very expensive in print. Not specifically an etymology dictionary.

### 53. Zhengzhang Shangfang (鄭張尚芳) -- 上古音系 (Old Chinese Phonological System)
- **Full title:** 上古音系 (Old Chinese Phonology)
- **Author:** Zhengzhang Shangfang (鄭張尚芳, 1933--2018)
- **Year:** Volume 1: 2003; Volume 2: 2013

The "Zhengzhang-Pan System" is one of the three major Chinese-developed OC reconstruction systems.
- **Scholarly reputation:** One of the most important Chinese-developed OC reconstruction systems, rivaling Baxter-Sagart in influence within Chinese linguistics.

### 54. Pan Wuyun (潘悟雲) -- 漢語歷史音韻學
- **Full title:** 漢語歷史音韻學 (Chinese Historical Phonology)
- **Author:** Pan Wuyun (潘悟雲, b. 1943)
- **Year:** 2000
- **Publisher:** Shanghai Educational Publishing House (上海教育出版社)
- **Etymology info:** Comprehensive treatment of Chinese historical phonology. Pan's most significant contribution is the proposal that Old Chinese "laryngeal" initials were actually uvular stops (*q-, *qh-, *G-). This proposal was adopted by both Zhengzhang Shangfang and Baxter-Sagart.
- **Scholarly reputation:** Highly influential in the field of historical Chinese phonology. The uvular stop proposal is now widely adopted.

### 55. Starostin, Sergei A. -- Old Chinese Reconstruction

- **Author:** Sergei Starostin (1953--2005)
- **Year:** 1989
- **Publisher:** Nauka, Moscow
- **Etymology info:** An independent Old Chinese reconstruction system developed from comparative Sino-Tibetan data. Starostin independently arrived at a six-vowel system similar to Zhengzhang and Baxter. His data is available through the Tower of Babel etymological database.
- **Digital availability:** Character readings are available on the [Tower of Babel](https://starlingdb.org/) (Starling database), compiled by Starostin himself from 1991 onward.
- **Scholarly reputation:** Widely respected reconstruction system. The Starling database remains an important freely-accessible resource for comparative Sino-Tibetan data.

  - STEDT database freely searchable at [stedt.berkeley.edu](https://stedt.berkeley.edu/)

- **Dong Zuobin (董作賓)** -- "甲骨文斷代研究例" (Studies in the Periodization of Oracle Bone Script, 1932). Established the five-period chronology for oracle bone inscriptions.
- **Takashima, Ken-ichi** -- "Towards a More Rigorous Methodology of Deciphering Oracle-Bone Inscriptions" (various papers). Available on [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/profile/Ken-Ichi-Takashima).

### 58. Notable Dissertations and Theses

- **Ash, John (Dr.)** -- Ph.D. dissertation on Chinese paleography and historical linguistics, National Taiwan Normal University. Formed the basis for the [Outlier Dictionary of Chinese Characters](https://www.outlier-linguistics.com/).
- **Xu Shushi** -- "An Analysis of Duan Yucai's Theory of Shengyi Tongyuan in his Annotations to the Shuowen Jiezi" (2024, University of Wales Trinity Saint David). Available at [UWTSD Repository](https://repository.uwtsd.ac.uk/id/eprint/3155/).

## VII. DIGITAL RESOURCES & DATABASES

### 59. ctext.org (Chinese Text Project)
- **URL:** [ctext.org](https://ctext.org/)
- **Content:** World's largest digital library of pre-modern Chinese. Over 30,000 titles, 5+ billion characters.
- **Available classical dictionaries:** Shuowen Jiezi (full text), Kangxi Dictionary (full text), Erya, Yupian, Guangyun, Shuowen Tongxun Dingsheng, and many others.
- **API:** [ctext.org/tools/api](https://ctext.org/tools/api) (JSON, rate-limited)
- **License:** Data is CC BY-NC-SA for wiki/scanned texts; classical texts are public domain.

### 60. Xiaoxuetang (小學堂) -- Academia Sinica
- **URL:** [xiaoxue.iis.sinica.edu.tw](https://xiaoxue.iis.sinica.edu.tw/)
- **Content:** The largest Chinese philology (文字學) database, maintained by Academia Sinica. Includes historical character forms, Guangyun data, Shuowen data, and phonological analysis.
- **Coverage:** 22,000+ characters with glyph evolution across multiple script periods.
- **Access:** Free web-based; registration may be required.

### 61. CHANT (Chinese Ancient Texts) Database
- **URL:** [CUHK D.C. Lau Research Centre](https://www.cuhk.edu.hk/ics/rccat/en/database.html)
- **Content:** Five databases covering oracle bone texts, bronze inscriptions, bamboo and silk texts, extant Han and pre-Han texts, and Weijin/Northern-Southern Dynasties texts.
- **Access:** Institutional subscription required at most universities.

### 62. Multi-function Chinese Character Database (漢語多功能字庫) -- CUHK
- **URL:** [humanum.arts.cuhk.edu.hk/Lexis/lexi-mf/](https://humanum.arts.cuhk.edu.hk/Lexis/lexi-mf/)
- **Content:** Etymological explanations, archaic script forms, componential trees, English-Chinese lookup. Based on Lin Yutang's dictionary enhanced with etymological and paleographic data.
- **Access:** Free web-based lookup; no bulk download.
- **Quality:** Rated "world leading" academically.

### 63. Hanziyuan (漢字源 / Chinese Etymology) -- Richard Sears
- **URL:** [hanziyuan.net](https://hanziyuan.net/)
- **Content:** 100,000+ ancient Chinese character forms including oracle bone, bronze, seal, Shuowen, and Liushutong characters. Searchable by character.
- **Source code:** [github.com/Dixin/Etymology](https://github.com/Dixin/Etymology)
- **Access:** Free web; glyph images NOT separately downloadable. Data not openly licensed.

### 64. Outlier Linguistics Dictionary
- **URL:** [outlier-linguistics.com](https://www.outlier-linguistics.com/)
- **Content:** A proprietary Chinese character etymology dictionary for iOS and Android, based on cutting-edge academic research. Expert Edition includes extensive historical information.
- **Methodology:** Developed by Dr. Ash (Ph.D. in Chinese paleography, National Taiwan Normal University). Distinguishes form, sound, and meaning components rather than using the traditional radical system.
- **Access:** Paid app; not open source.
- **Quality:** "Very High" -- based on the latest paleographic research; uses academic sources unavailable elsewhere in English.

## VIII. ADDITIONAL REFERENCE WORKS OF NOTE

### 68. DeFrancis, John ed. -- ABC Chinese-English Comprehensive Dictionary
- **Year:** 2003
- **Publisher:** University of Hawaii Press
- **ISBN:** 978-0-824-82766-3
- **Coverage:** 196,501+ entries
- **Notes:** Pioneering alphabetical (pinyin-sorted) dictionary. Makes the first clear distinction between etymology of words and evolution of character shapes/sounds/meanings. Available in Pleco app.

### 69. Henshall, Kenneth G. -- "A Guide to Remembering Japanese Characters"
- **Year:** 1988 (1st ed.); updated editions available
- **Publisher:** Tuttle Publishing
- **ISBN:** 978-0-804-82038-2
- **Coverage:** 2,000+ joyo kanji with historical-etymological essays
- **Notes:** Provides brief historical development essays and then mnemonic suggestions. Relies heavily on Kato Joken's methodology. Good for early pictographic forms but mnemonics sometimes diverge from true etymology.

### 70. Zhengzhang Shangfang -- 上古音系 (see entry #53)
### 71. Pan Wuyun -- 漢語歷史音韻學 (see entry #54)

### 72. Dong Zuobin (董作賓) -- Oracle Bone Inscription Research
- **Major work:** 甲骨文斷代研究例 (Studies in the Periodization of Oracle Bone Script), 1932
- **Contribution:** Established the five-period chronology for oracle bone inscriptions, creating dating standards that are still followed by scholars.
- **Related work:** Co-directed the first archaeological excavation at Anyang (1928).

### 73. Li Xueqin (李學勤, 1933--2019) -- Collected Works
- **Full title:** 李學勤文集 (Collected Works of Li Xueqin), 30 volumes
- **Publisher:** Various; Shanghai Guji Chubanshe and others
- **Contributions:** Published 40+ books and 1,000+ papers on oracle bone inscriptions, bronze inscriptions, bamboo and silk manuscripts, and ancient Chinese civilization. Led compilation of Mawangdui silk texts, Shuihudi Qin bamboo texts, and Tsinghua bamboo slips.
- **Methodology:** Combined history, textual studies, archaeology, paleography, and art history, emphasizing how ancient texts and archaeology corroborate each other.
- **Scholarly reputation:** One of the most important Chinese scholars of the 20th century in the field of ancient text studies and paleography.
