# Independent check: 学 / 學 grouped form, 子, and sound role

Research conducted 2026-09-27. This is an independently sourced note, not publication approval for either Chinese 学 or Japanese 学. I did not obtain or inspect 黃德寬主編《古文字譜系疏證》(商務印書館, 2007), vol. 4, pp. 469–470. The Outlier/Pleco bibliography lead in [outlier-xue-pipeline-lessons.md](outlier-xue-pipeline-lessons.md) therefore remains a citation lead only. ## Evidence and exact graph scope

1. **CUHK, Multi-function Chinese Character Database, traditional 學 entry, “形義通解”** — https://humanum.arts.cuhk.edu.hk/Lexis/lexi-mf/search.php?word=%E5%AD%B8. Directly inspected the entry text online. It reports the character-level account: oracle forms may comprise 𦥑 (two hands), 五 and 六, or omit 五 and use 爻 as phonetic; it interprets the first as hands manipulating counting sticks / divining, and says bronze forms add 子, related to children and education. The same entry explicitly labels as an alternative 白川靜’s reading of 爻 + 宀 as a roof with crossed timbers / school building. It also cites 沈培 for the child/learning interpretation and 季旭昇 for the semantic/grammatical 教 vs 學 distinction. These are source-reported scholarly positions, not all separately verified here. Crucially, the claim “爻 is phonetic” is conditional on specified oracle form variants, not a role established for every 學 graph. CUHK displays oracle, bronze, Warring States, bamboo-slip and seal-script glyph samples on this exact traditional entry, but its prose's chronology assertion of 子 appearing in bronze inscriptions needs checking against glyph-level specimens and fuller palaeographic literature.

2. **Taiwan Ministry of Education, Dictionary of Chinese Character Variants, 學** — https://dict.variants.moe.edu.tw/dictView.jsp?educode=A01001. Directly inspected. Its transcription of *Shuowen Jiezi* says the seal form 斆 means “awakening/understanding,” is formed from 敎 and 冖 (“still unenlightened”), with 𦥑 phonetic; 學 is an abbreviated seal form of 斆. This records Xu Shen's Han-era analysis, not direct evidence for Shang oracle graph structure. The entry's modern standard-form description identifies the central top strokes as 爻 and lower element as 子; useful for current-form identification. The website lists but marks its oracle-bronze reference plates unavailable, so it does not provide inspected oracle specimens here.

3. **CUHK, 学 simplified entry** — https://humanum.arts.cuhk.edu.hk/Lexis/lexi-mf/search.php?word=%E5%AD%A6. Directly inspected. Establishes the database's mapping of simplified 学 to traditional 學 and current pronouncing/lexical data; it does not itself explain simplification genealogy. Taiwan MOE's 学 variant page https://dict.variants.moe.edu.tw/dictView.jsp?ID=10539&powerMode=2&q=1 directly labels 学 as a variant of 學 and cites a comparison of officially promulgated simplified forms. For historical stages, this is insufficient to assign the current top shape to an ancient semantic/phonetic function. Explain simplified 学 synchronically as retaining 子 beneath an abbreviated upper shape; leave the detailed graphic path open pending a reliable source on forms and standardization.

4. **CUHK usage guide** — https://humanum.arts.cuhk.edu.hk/Lexis/lexi-mf/guide.php. Directly inspected in search results. It explains the database's conventions, including hyphens/brackets for components and its own meaning of “形義源流通解.” Useful to interpret the CUHK entry but not independent palaeographic support.

5. **Guoxuedashi glyph chronology page, 学** — https://www.guoxuedashi.com/zixing/yanbian/2955ea/. Directly inspected, but this is an aggregator/transcription rather than a primary edition. It indexes three oracle examples, two bronze examples, Warring States and Qin slips, seal and later forms; its prose says one oracle form has 宀 and a reduced 爻, a second has an uncertain element with 爻, and a third combines uncertain element, 宀 and reduced 爻, treating these as phonosemantic. This usefully demonstrates variant structure and disagreement, but should not be cited as decisive evidence absent verification against the named glyph corpora and underlying reference list.

## Assessment

- **Current grouping:** In traditional 學, the learner-visible upper unit 𦥯 / 𦥑-like group is a legitimate grouped form worth explaining; 子 is visibly and independently recognized as the child component in the lower position. Do not flatten the upper structure into presumed individual current pieces and project those identities unchanged into oracle forms. The exact relationship of the modern upper grouping to particular oracle variants needs specimen-level checking.
- **子 across time:** CUHK's account specifically says 子 was added in bronze forms; its own displayed early oracle examples and component prose indicate that a child is not required in all earlier forms. Therefore describe 子 as explicit in later/traditional forms, and avoid saying that every ancient graph has a child. Directly verify which inscriptions/forms contain it before a stronger chronological statement.
- **Sound role:** A phonetic role for 爻 has affirmative support in CUHK's described oracle variant(s), and in *Shuowen* as transmitted by the MOE lexicon, which assigns 𦥑 as the phonetic element for 斆, not 爻. These are different analyses, graphs and periods. CUHK's attributed competing reading emphasizes schoolhouse semantics, not phonetics. The claimed role should therefore be narrowly phrased: some analyses/forms treat 爻 in particular oracle spellings as a phonetic element; do not call all of the grouped upper part a phonetic component, nor infer a phonetic role for 子 or modern 学's reduced top. To make the sound claim publication-ready, cite and inspect the form(s) and obtain the phonological comparison/reconstruction used by the source. Mandarin xué vs yáo is not a relevant falsifier or proof by itself.
- **Additional local book support, with scope:** Li Xueqin's directly inspected 《字源》 entry (2012, printed p. 265) presents a phonetic 爻 analysis as one of two oracle-stage structural analyses; its other analysis is semantic, involving hands and a learning place. It varies by the pictured early form and does not warrant assigning the sound function to the whole later grouped top or current simplified 学. This strengthens the case that the pipeline should preserve multiple role assignments scoped to actual graph variants. It remains one author's account; cited corpora/plates in its bibliography have not been independently inspected here.
- **Competing formation analyses:** CUHK's preferred account connects forms containing hand(s), numerical/divinatory marks and sometimes a child to teaching/learning; it records variant decomposition and the schoolhouse interpretation. The question whether 五/六/爻 is the right identification, whether a given sign is phonetic or semantic, and what the earliest scene represents remains graph-variant-specific. Present as alternatives and scope each to forms/authors, not as universal truths. *Shuowen*'s account belongs to the seal graph 斆 and its derivative 學 and must be marked as later received analysis.
- **Outlier lead:** The cited 2007 book pages remain unread. Neither CUHK's account nor its references establish what Huang says on pp. 469–470. Obtain the book or scan and compare its cited specimens/argument with CUHK and the palaeographic corpus before incorporating that specific attribution.

## Small contrastive smoke subset

- **Positional form:** 氵 in 江, supported by CUHK usage guide (“江” from 氵 (= 水) as semantic sign, 工 sound). This tests positional variant/full-form identity plus role distinction. Entry: https://humanum.arts.cuhk.edu.hk/Lexis/lexi-mf/search.php?word=%E6%B1%9F.
- **Replacement/abbreviation contrast:** simplified 汉 corresponding to 漢 is a useful deliberate replacement contrast: modern 又 is not the ancient graph's inherited component role, and simplified replacement must not be called positional variant. For a strict evidence-driven smoke case, source 漢 via CUHK and the official standardization history (PRC State Council decision: https://www.moe.gov.cn/jyb_xwfb/xw_fbh/moe_2128/moe_2326/moe_1144/tnull_14350.html); inspect the underlying 漢 history before asserting which historical part the replacement displaced. Alternatively 親→亲 provides a clear abbreviation but is a less useful sound-role contrast. Do not infer semantic “hand” function of 又 in 汉 merely from its glyph.

## Pipeline lessons

The main failure class is **component granularity and scope drift**: writers can correctly identify a current grouped shape but fail to research it as a group; or flatten historical variant glyphs into present-day radicals and assign one role universally. Both CUHK and the directly inspected 《字源》 entry show that analysis can vary across graph variants and scholarly authors. Apply the existing pipeline rule to require (a) graph-level specimen provenance, (b) a grouped-component explanation before stroke-level decomposition, (c) explicit distinction among present identity, historical interpretation and proposed continuity, and (d) exact scope for phonetic and competing readings. Keep the competing account in expert detail and make a concise supported learner account. Treat the citation in Pleco as bibliography metadata only until pp. 469–470 are actually read. The small smoke check should compare 学/學 with 江 (positional 氵) and 汉/漢 (replacement), so the updated rule does not erase legitimate modern component identities or transfer obsolete roles to simplified shapes. Reuse the acquired-books index and page tools when they locate an exact headword; do not treat a neighbouring pilot page or title-level availability as character-specific evidence. The local 《字源》 discovery is useful direct evidence; 《說文新證》 has not yet yielded a directly inspected 學 entry.
