I inspected the scan pixels; corpus OCR is only a locator/transcription lead. The headword is 備(备), with forms labeled Western Zhou, Warring States, 《说文》古文 and 小篆, Han, traditional regular, and simplified regular script. The author calls 備 phonosemantic, quotes 《说文》 as “備，慎也。从人，[printed phonetic graph]声,” and describes an arrow-container/quiver account. The author says 人 remained visible, sound-bearing structure became obscured through graphic change, and simplified 备 was selected from a cursive form of the sound element and fixed as a whole graph. This supports a source-specific account of whole-form simplification; it does not identify the rare printed phonetic graph as 𤰈, 葡, or 蒲, nor establish historical roles for the present visible parts 夂 and 田.

The article research keeps this account distinct from the received 人 + 𤰈 analysis and from the competing bronze whole-quiver interpretation. Their relationship remains unresolved. Four rare printed occurrences on this page remain occurrence references p001–p004; do not merge their identities or infer Unicode readings. The book’s cited 《甲金篆》 p.545 and 《说文》 p.163 were not separately inspected in this enrichment.

## Additional research

A separate research-first enrichment inspected the contemporary 汉典 entry [备 bèi - 汉典](https://zdic.net/hans/%E5%A4%87) (ZDIC), accessed 2026-10-02. Its basic explanation lists 具备/完备 (“have; complete”), 预备/准备 (“prepare”), and 设备 (“equipment”); its examples include 防备. These directly support the entry’s current “have; complete,” “prepare,” and “equipment” senses. The same page’s detailed lexicographic section lists “cautious/careful” and “all/entirely” with quotations from older works (including 《大戴礼记》, 《墨子》, 《礼记》, and 《左传》); this documents historical citations but does not establish whether those senses remain in current use. Do not expand the current sense “have; complete” into a separate “ready/equipped” claim without evidence for that exact scope. This is a useful current-use source, but not an official PRC dictionary. Taiwan Ministry entries give modern Mandarin bèi for 備 and 𤰈; no matched historical reconstruction was verified in one named reconstruction system. The modern reading match alone does not prove the ancient sound relation.
