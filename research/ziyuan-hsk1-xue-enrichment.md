## What the 学 entry reports

The page attributes several distinct analyses to the entry's author, 張標. It describes associative 商 forms as 升 + 冖 or 林 + 冖. Its phonosemantic proposal identifies 爻 as the sound element and 升 or 冖 as the other element; it compares 爻's appearance to interwoven objects (交织) and says a reduced form may omit half as 乂. A separate proposal uses a rare element read jù and described as two hands or claws for both sound and meaning. Further forms combine the source's unusual element with 爻 and/or 冖. The glyphs for that element and its reductions are retained as scan-bound drawings; their Unicode identities are not guessed.

The account says these structures began combining in 商 forms and proposes that 子 was added in 西周 forms to mark the child learner. It reports reciprocal use of 學 and 教, learning and imitation/efficacy as original meanings, and later uses for school, instruction, knowledge and academic work. It separately reports 學 written for other words. These are the book's attributed analyses and examples, not a settled reconstruction or a complete semantic chronology. The page does not establish that the proposed historical function of 子 continued as an independent function in modern 学.

The scan clearly prints 爻 as the sound element. The parenthetical 交织 describes a crossed/interwoven appearance; it does not identify 交 as the phonetic component. This differs from the separate 教 entry immediately above, where the book's account names 交 as a descriptive form and 爻 as its sound. Keep those neighboring headwords separate.

The separate CUHK 学 entry identifies 學 as its standardized traditional counterpart and directs readers to the 學 account; it does not supply a distinct etymological analysis of the modern upper strokes. Thus the visible upper assembly can be described without assigning ⺍, 冖, roof, or sound roles from the traditional graph. 子 is visible at the bottom. Keep that current graphic observation apart from the attributed Western Zhou proposal.
