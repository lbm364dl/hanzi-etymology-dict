## What the book says

**教 (author credit: 張標).** It classifies the character as phonosemantic. Its account says the Shang oracle-bone form is 攴 with 爻 as phonetic: 攴 depicts an action with a hand-held implement and relates to teaching; 爻 depicts crossed/interwoven objects and is the sound element. It proposes that 子 was later added to highlight education of children, yielding 攴 + 子 + 爻. It traces the dominant 教 form through later periods, while noting an early 爻-reduced variant persisted into Warring States texts and that Warring States forms can omit 攴 and write 孝. It reports cross-use between 学 and 教 in bronze/manuscript contexts, including a 靜簋 “學” read as 教 and a 楚簡 教 read as 学, and describes ancient teaching/learning as sharing a graph or word before later differentiation. It gives teaching/education/order as senses read jiào, and “impart; cause; let” for jiāo, alongside loan uses and a Shang place-name use. These are 李學勤主編《字源》的 analyses and reported examples, not settled consensus.

**學 (author credit: 張標).** The account says early forms include both associative and phonosemantic structures. For the former it interprets oracle-bone variants as 升 (gǒng; described as two hands) + 冖 (a building/learning place), or 林 + 冖. For phonosemantic forms it explicitly says 爻 is the sound element in one analysis, with a semantic element printed 升 or 冖; 爻 can be reduced by half to 乂. The parenthetical describes 爻 as an interwoven/crossed form, using 交 to describe that shape; 交 is not named as the phonetic element here. A second analysis uses a page-specific glyph, corpus reference `[glyph:g022]`, read jù and described as “two hands or two claws,” as both sound and semantic element. The scan text also describes variants with two of this element and 冖, and a reduced-form glyph `[glyph:g023]`; I retain these source-bound glyph references rather than guessing their Unicode identities. The book says 爻 and the jù element have related ancient velar initials and that the structures began combining in Shang forms, including combinations with 爻, with or without 冖, and one with reduced 爻. It treats later 西周 forms as adding 子 to mark the child learner, and traces form changes through the simplified modern 学. It says the original meaning is learning and imitation/efficacy; cites school, learning, teaching and instruction uses, including use of 學 for 教 and 教 for 學; and reports a Han use of 學 for huá (a borrowing), not a semantic extension of “learning.” The entry notes readings xué, jiào and huá. This is a distinctly articulated source account; keep its alternatives attributed rather than collapsing them into one component story.

## OCR issues confirmed against the scans

- On PDF p. 276, in the 教 account, the OCR reads **「声旁爻像器物交织形」** while the scan prints **「声旁交像器物交织形」**. The source elsewhere in that account identifies 爻 as the sound element; retain the printed 交 in this descriptive line and do not silently normalize the apparent tension.
- On PDF p. 277, in the separate 學 account, the scan explicitly prints **「一种以爻为声」** and later **「爻或省略一半作乂」**. The OCR agrees on 爻 there. Its parenthetical compares 爻 with 交; that wording is not a phonetic analysis with 交. I had previously transferred the separate 教-page 交/爻 issue into the 學 analysis; the scan does not support that.
- On PDF p. 277 the OCR contains the stray Latin string **“heat”** in a Chinese sentence (“所以在字形上也 heat有所体现”); the scan reads **“所以在字形上也有所体现”**. This is a plainly inserted OCR artifact, not book text.
- Historical-form labels are mostly represented by opaque `[glyph:…]` placeholders in the corpus. They locate illustrations but do not expose their pixels or justify assigning a Unicode identity from OCR alone. For this check, the displayed form sequences and headword identities were read from the source scans.

These examples confirm two distinct failure classes: plausible OCR can alter a component name in the exact argument being researched, and adjacent headword accounts can contaminate one another during synthesis even after scans have been opened. Unrelated inserted text can also survive in otherwise fluent Chinese. Search hits should remain leads; agents need to inspect the exact passage and keep each headword's claims separate.

## Relation to the current approved entries

- **学**: 《字源》 adds a material, attributable analysis that is absent from the current entry: the proposed 升/林 + 冖 associative forms; a phonosemantic analysis with 爻 as sound and 升/冖 as semantic; a second analysis using the page-specific jù glyph as both sound and semantic; their combination in Shang forms; and the detailed relation to 教. The current article's two summary alternatives (hands/tallies and 爻 + roof) do not represent this source's full account. This is additive evidence and may require broadening the account of competing scholarship; it does not by itself disprove the current source-attributed proposals or settle the graphic path. The scan directly supports the book's relevant sentences, while its ancient-form identities and interpretation remain the book's scholarship.
- **教**: The current entry already covers 攴, 爻, disputed 子 timing, interchanges with 学, and competing analyses. 《字源》 independently and directly supports 攴 + 爻 and later 子, and adds historical details about the reduced/abbreviated forms, reciprocal 学/教 use, and reading/sense distinctions. It materially strengthens the dossier and could refine the history, but does not overturn the current cautious framing. Its statement that teaching and learning shared a graph/word should remain a sourced proposal alongside the contrary article already represented in the dossier.
